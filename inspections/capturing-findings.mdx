Findings are the core of your inspection report. Each finding represents a specific defect, observation, or item you want to call out — from a cracked ceiling joist to a missing GFCI outlet to a water heater nearing the end of its service life. InspectIQ gives you a fast, structured way to capture findings on-site so your report is thorough, consistent, and ready to deliver.

<Note>
  Findings are the primary content of your final report. Every finding you record — complete with its description, severity rating, photos, and location — will appear in the appropriate section of the delivered report. Taking a moment to capture each one completely on-site saves significant editing time later.
</Note>

## What Is a Finding?

A finding is any specific item you want to document during an inspection. This could be:

- A **defect** that requires repair or replacement (for example, damaged flashing at the chimney)
- An **observation** that isn't a defect but is worth noting (for example, a previous repair that appears complete)
- A **maintenance item** the homeowner should address to prevent future issues (for example, caulking around a bathtub)
- A **safety concern** that needs immediate attention (for example, exposed electrical wiring)

Each finding lives within a specific area of the inspection and can include a title, description, severity level, photos, and location details.

## Adding a New Finding

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Relevant Area">
    From the area list, tap the area where the finding is located — for example, **Kitchen** or **Attic**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tap 'Add Finding'">
    Scroll down past the area overview section and tap **+ Add Finding**. A new finding form opens.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter or Select a Title">
    Type a title for the finding, or tap **Browse Library** to choose from a pre-written entry in the finding library. If you select a library item, the title and description fields will auto-fill with standard language you can customize.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the Severity">
    Tap the **Severity** field and select the appropriate level:
    - **Major Defect** — A significant issue that affects the safety, function, or structural integrity of the home and typically requires prompt professional attention.
    - **Minor Defect** — A defect that should be repaired but does not pose an immediate safety risk or structural concern.
    - **Maintenance Item** — A routine upkeep task that, if left unaddressed, could develop into a more serious issue.
    - **Note** — An observation, disclosure, or informational item that doesn't require action but is worth documenting.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write a Description">
    In the **Description** field, describe the finding in clear, plain language. Explain what you observed, where it is located within the area, and — when appropriate — what you recommend. You can type or use voice-to-text.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add Photos">
    Tap the **camera icon** in the Photos section to capture images of the finding, or tap **Choose from Library** to import existing photos from your camera roll. Add as many photos as needed, and annotate them to point out specific concerns.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the Location">
    If the finding is tied to a specific spot within the area (for example, the northeast corner of the basement or the second-floor hallway ceiling), tap the **Location** field and add a brief note.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the Finding">
    Tap **Save** or tap the back arrow to return to the area screen. Your finding is saved automatically and appears in the finding list for that area.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Using the Finding Library

The finding library is a built-in collection of pre-written titles and descriptions for the most common defects and observations inspectors encounter. Using it saves time and helps you maintain consistent, professional language across every report.

<Tip>
  Get in the habit of checking the finding library before typing a description from scratch. Search by keyword — like "flashing," "GFCI," or "efflorescence" — and you'll often find a ready-made entry that fits perfectly. You can always edit the pre-filled text to match the specific conditions you observed.
</Tip>

To search the library while adding a finding:
1. Tap **Browse Library** from the finding form.
2. Type a keyword into the search bar or browse by category (Electrical, Plumbing, Roof, etc.).
3. Tap any entry to preview the title and default description.
4. Tap **Use This Finding** to populate the form with that entry's content.

You can also add your own custom entries to the library for defects you encounter frequently. See the **Finding Library** settings guide for instructions.

## Duplicating a Finding

If the same defect appears in multiple areas — for example, damaged window caulking in both the living room and the master bedroom — you don't have to create the finding from scratch each time.

1. Open the finding you want to duplicate.
2. Tap the **options menu** (three dots) in the top-right corner.
3. Select **Duplicate Finding**.
4. In the dialog that appears, choose the area (or areas) where you want to copy the finding.
5. Tap **Duplicate**. An identical copy of the finding appears in each selected area, where you can edit the description or photos as needed to reflect the specific conditions in that space.

## Deleting or Archiving a Finding

**To delete a finding:**
1. Open the finding.
2. Tap the **options menu** (three dots) and select **Delete Finding**.
3. Confirm the deletion. Deleted findings are permanently removed and cannot be recovered.

**To archive a finding:**
Archiving lets you hide a finding from the report without deleting it permanently — useful if you're unsure whether an item warrants inclusion and want to decide later.

1. Open the finding.
2. Tap the **options menu** and select **Archive Finding**.
3. The finding is hidden from the active findings list but can be viewed and restored from the **Archived** filter at any time during the inspection.

<Warning>
  Deleting a finding is permanent. If there's any chance you might want the information later, use **Archive** instead so you can restore it before completing the inspection.
</Warning>
